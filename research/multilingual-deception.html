Ongoing research

Multilingual Deception Detection

Understanding when AI-generated text detection methods can—and cannot—be trusted.

Multilingual NLPLLM EvaluationAI Reliability

Overview & Motivation

As language models become better at producing natural text, it's increasingly important to understand how reliably AI-generated content can be distinguished from human writing. Yet detection performance measured on one language or model family may not transfer to others. This research looks beyond headline accuracy figures to study generalization, evaluation reliability, and sources of model failure.

Research Questions

  1. How does detection performance differ across languages and linguistic contexts?
  2. How well do detectors generalize to text produced by previously unseen language-model families?
  3. How do classical classifiers and LLM-based judges differ in their reliability and failure modes?

Methodology

The project uses multilingual human-written text and controlled AI-generated counterparts to create comparable evaluation conditions. We test classical machine-learning approaches alongside LLM-based evaluations, including variations in prompting and model family.

We examine performance across language–generator combinations, and use reproducible experimental configurations and reliability checks so that comparisons remain meaningful.

Current Progress

Experiments and analysis are ongoing. This page will be updated with research findings, visualizations, and public resources when they are ready to share.

My Contributions

My work includes developing reproducible data-generation and evaluation pipelines, designing controlled comparisons, and analyzing how performance changes across languages, model families, and evaluation settings.
