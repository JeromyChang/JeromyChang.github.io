Completed research project

GRADE: Generalizable Reasoning-Aware Dialogue Evaluation for AI Tutors

Evaluating what makes AI-generated teaching feedback useful—not just correct.

LLM EvaluationEducational NLPReasoning & Fine-Tuning

Overview & Motivation

AI tutors need to do more than give mathematically correct answers. A helpful response should recognize when a student makes a mistake, identify where it happened, guide the student toward a correction, and offer a meaningful next step. GRADE investigates how well open-source language models can evaluate these aspects of pedagogical quality in student–tutor dialogue.

Research Questions

  1. How do model scale and fine-tuning affect assessments of tutor feedback?
  2. Is chain-of-thought reasoning more useful during evaluation or when generating training data?
  3. Can one multitask model assess all four pedagogical dimensions as effectively as separate models?

Methodology

We evaluate five open-source language models in 120 experimental configurations. The experiments compare zero-shot inference with LoRA fine-tuning, synthetic data generation with and without self-verification, reasoning-enabled settings, and single-task versus multitask prediction.

Each tutor response is assessed on mistake identification, mistake location, providing guidance, and actionability. We use strict macro-F1 as the main performance measure, and also examine resource usage and carbon emissions.

Key Findings

  • Model and task choices matter. The strongest single-task configuration differs from the strongest multitask configuration.
  • Augmentation is not universally helpful. It improves some weaker or constrained models but produces smaller gains for models already performing well.
  • Reasoning has different effects at different stages. In this setting, it proved more useful for generating additional data than for direct classification.
  • Verification introduces trade-offs. Extra self-verification substantially increases cost without consistently improving performance.

My Contributions

In this collaborative NLP term research project, I worked on extending the evaluation setup to all four pedagogical dimensions, comparing open-source model configurations, and analyzing how reasoning, fine-tuning, and augmentation affected performance.
