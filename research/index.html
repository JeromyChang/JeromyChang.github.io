Research

My work focuses on understanding how AI systems behave, how we can evaluate their reliability, and what their failures reveal. These projects explore those questions in different settings.

NLP

Ongoing research

Multilingual Deception Detection

Many evaluations of AI-generated text detection focus on a single language or model. This project takes a broader view, comparing classical machine learning detectors and LLM-based judgments across languages and generators. By studying both familiar and unseen conditions, I aim to understand how well these methods generalize and which evaluation choices make their reported performance more—or less—trustworthy.

GRADE

Completed research project

GRADE: Generalizable Reasoning-Aware Dialogue Evaluation for AI Tutors

An AI tutor can produce a correct answer without providing useful teaching. GRADE examines how open-source language models evaluate tutoring responses across four dimensions: mistake identification, mistake location, guidance, and actionability. We compare five models and 120 experimental configurations to study the trade-offs among model scale, LoRA fine-tuning, reasoning, synthetic augmentation, multitask learning, and computational cost.
